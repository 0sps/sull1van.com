# Dashboard Quiet Luxury Design

## Goal

Refine the FE Study home dashboard into a calmer, more beautiful landing page that uses depth with restraint rather than obvious effect styling.

## Approved Direction

- Move away from overt neomorphic signaling and toward a quiet-luxury dashboard.
- Keep the warm palette and tactile undertone, but remove decorative accents that feel forced.
- Eliminate the small multicolor bars from the recommendation cards.

## Design Corrections

- Reduce visible bevels, inner rims, and ornamental gradients.
- Let typography, spacing, and tonal hierarchy carry more of the refinement.
- Flatten the sidebar support modules slightly so the topic cards remain the true focal surfaces.
- Make buttons feel sharper and calmer, with less puffiness.

## Component Strategy

### Hero

- Simplify the hero into a single elegant paper-like plane.
- Keep the serif heading, but let it live in more open space with less surrounding treatment.
- Make the saved-quiz surface read as a subtle status plaque rather than a decorative badge.

### Stats

- Integrate stat cards into the hero more quietly.
- Remove ornamental accent bars and reduce novelty styling.
- Preserve clarity and contrast while making the tiles feel more architectural.

### Sidebar

- Rework sidebar panels as soft support trays rather than sculpted objects.
- Make recommendation cards feel like clean inserts with no decorative top stripes.
- Preserve warmth, but reduce competing visual noise.

### Topic Cards

- Keep topic cards as the main content surfaces.
- Soften the green header wash and remove any exaggerated plate effect.
- Make subtopic rows feel more like clean paper slips than separate raised widgets.

## UX Intent

The page should feel collected, poised, and premium:

1. The hero sets tone with confidence rather than spectacle.
2. Sidebar content supports the page quietly.
3. Topic cards carry the strongest visual emphasis through scale and clarity, not ornament.

## Out of Scope

- Quiz/testing surfaces
- Interaction model changes
- Data or information architecture changes
