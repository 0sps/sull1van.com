One full year! Crazy to say it. Today officially marks one year since I went sober. 



## The rules of my sobriety

Going sober was pretty daunting at first. I didn't really know what was in the bounds versus out of the bounds of being sober. It sounds easy on the surface, but technicalities quickly arise. For example, what if I drink a "Non-alcoholic" beer that actually has 0.1% or 0.5% alcohol? 

After some thinking, I decided that the goal of my sobriety wasn't to win a war with the universe and to banish all traces of alcohol molecules from my body. Instead, it was to avoid consuming anything that would get me drunk. To that end, I made some rules:
1. If it's labeled "NA", meaning 0.5% or less, I could drink it.
2. If something had true alcohol, I counted it as breaking sobriety if I swallowed it. Dipping my tongue to taste a friend's espresso martini is fine.


You might be saying these are permissive rules, and I'd say that's fine. They worked for me. I was never in the territory of life-or-death alcoholism, so I didn't feel like my sobriety needed to be as strict. Plus, the amount consumed from an NA Corona at 0.1% alcohol is so little, you can't feel it. That's why they call them NA.


Another tenant of my sobriety that it was actually threefold. It included **weed, nicotine, and alcohol**. To be honest, I don't know of anyone who went sober from all 3 at the same time. But I do know of plenty of people who don't do any of these things, so I'm not special. I just wanted to make it more of a true sobriety for me since I knew I would get high every once in a while. The nicotine part was just added bonus, I've never been into nicotine so going sober wasn't a challenge.


So while my sobriety was threefold, it was only acoohol that scared me. I told my parents and my freidns that I was doing it for two months, just to see where things go. I didn't commit to longer because I didn't think I could **do** it any longer. 

As I neared the halfway mark, I caught up with a friend and received what might be the single greatest piece of advice I heard throughout this entire journey. I told him the situation, and that I was going sober for two months.

He asked why I had settled on two months. 

"I don't kmow," I said, "I just thought that's the most I could do."

"Why don't you just try to go longer?"


Sometimes you really just need someone to say the obvious thing.

Just go longer. That's exactly what I did. As I approached the two month mark, I said I would extend to six months. At six months, I was convinced I could do twelve. And here we are, twelve months later. It's crazy.



Some reflections on this past year:

1. **Going sober isn't for everyone, and I honestly wouldn't recommend it long term.** There is something to be said about having the opportunity to use substances when you need them. For example, a sweet treat every once in a while. A three-coffee day. A drink or two while you're out at the bar. I think that, for most people, you stand more to lose than to gain if you give up drinking for the rest of your life. Personally I have no desire to smoke weed or take nicotine, but alcohol is something I do miss. Going out on dates is a little more somber, and nights at the bars with friends have a more flat feeling to them. I don't miss being **drunk**, I miss the nice buzz that comes after two drinks. I know what's waiting for me at the bottom of a bottle, and it's not good. But near the top, just a shot or two in, THAT is the state that I miss. It's electric. I plan to return to it one day.

2. **Alcohol cravings are real, but they go away after three months.** By month three I no longer had the constant urge to drink. I'd settled in to the reality that I would go to the bar with my friends, sit my ass down, and order that soda or NA beer. Honestly the lack of alcohol feels more like the addition of 'boring' into my life than having a craving now.

3.**NA Beers are awesome. If you aren't a beer person, you might be after you go sober.** Look, they're not for everyone. Especially if you are the type of person who might be swayed to drink MORE if you go back to regular beer. But I love the concept of NA beers. They've come out in droves this past year, and I'm seeing more and more bars carry them. They make it easy to join my friends and have something to hold that's not a soda.

4. **You realize that most times you drank, you didn't need to.** Again, I don't miss being drunk. The nights I go out with my friends and DD while they get trashed, I legitimiately have no desire to redo that night and drink. I just miss those nice moments that happen about once a month, where you're at someone's house or maybe at a bar, and one drink would be perfect to soften the mood. THAT'S what I miss. But the vast majority of drinking, no. Good riddance.

5. **People constantly ask questions. Some of those people are supportive.** Some being the operating word here. I've had tons and tons of questions about my sobriety, and never once had someone shame me for it. But what I do get is a feeling of guilt, sometimes, where I see other people drinking and they almost seem upset that I'm not. Like if you went out to eat for a friend's birthday but brought your own food to the restaurant becasue you're on a meal plan. It just breaks social norms and confuses people sometimes. The alternative is to stay home and not go to social events, but that's a terrible alternative.

6. **There are some bodily changes, but you notice them within 3 months.** I noticed increased energy during the day and whitened eyes. Honestly, a great combo to have. I love how white my eyes are now.

7. **Some people encouraged me to drink when I was trying to drink less, and they are noticeably silent now that I'm sober.** This is probably the biggest thing to watch out for. I had explained my desire to drink less to everyone around me, and most people were supportive, but there were still a lot of people who would text me and ask to get extremely drunk with me. I'm not mad, that's what I was known for in college. But it's a weird feeling to be wanting to push yourself in one direction after being known to be the complete opposite, and the people around you aren't necessarily used to / serious about your new direction. I don't hold it against anyone, they aren't beholden to supporting me in any way. Plus, I didn't make it a big thing when I went sober, and I didn't really tell anyone how important it was for me. I think it comes in varying degrees though. There are some people who were extremely close to me at the time, who heard about my goal to go sober, and my growing frustration with drinking, and they encouraged me to go on plans and trips that were centered around drinking, to the point where it would be seen as a little out of place if I was the only one sober. No shade to anyone, these are just thougths as I'm writing.


what other thing that comes to mind is the countless number of times people heard about what I was doing and would say something like "that's so cool," or "I want to do that myself one day." I think that's totally valid, and you should! I just wouldn't recommend doing an entire 12 months for no particular reason. Honestly, what I did was a little overkill, and I wouldn't recommend it to most people. From what I've seen, most people have a reasonable relationship with alcohol, and they drink it situation. I think sobriety is a useful tool for anybody wanting better results, but that for a lot of people their lives should be harder if they did a full stop on drinking for this long of a time. Something easier could be, only drinking on Friday nights, or, stopping two drinks and never go past. That's easy for me to say, but not easy for me to do, so I want cold turkey.


One last sandwich to mention was just the sheer number of people who were incredibly supportive. People who I'd go out with repeatedly, and they would start to buy me NA beers. People who remembered, essentially. and honestly people who were just peefextly comortbake gojng out with me.




Will I stay sober past one year? As of right now, I haven't broken it. But that could change. I really achieved everything I was looking for during this year of sobriety, and I don't have anything left to prove to myself by going longer. It was more about convincing myself that I *could* do this, and that I could do it again if needed. Keep in mind, this decision was made in haste one day, when I woke up slightly hungover and absolutely pissed at myself for getting drunk the night before due to peer pressure. So now I know that if I can make a decision like this that easily, I can do it again. It's also been incredible to see just how much I could accomplish in the in-between when I'm not going out on weekends. I refined my self-driving car project, I honed my health and achieved the greated blood test results of my entire life, I worked on a charger project and a half dozen AI-assited vibe coding projects, all in all a lot of stuff. I'm excited to see what comes next.
