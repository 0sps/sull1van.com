This article will be about how I designed a workout app and a lifting routine to tackle my most difficult challenge yet, which is staying consistent at the gym.

<br>

I knew I needed to get better at lifting, and I hated that my progress had stalled. but ultimately, i jsut didnt' have the same pasiosn for the gym as i once did.

I started thinking deeply about what had changed. I had fallen off from lifting and cardio last fall (fall of 2025), and I didn't really want to do any of it again.

i even created my own workout tracker in november/december of 2025 to custom track my lifts to my specifications. However, while it DOES track lifts, I couldn't stop having the same issue with my lifts.

I'd go to the gym, do 1-2 lifts, then feel like I wasn't getting anything done and promptly go back home. Plus, every time I tracked a lift, it felt bad because I wasn't hitting good weight compared to my historical highs. I was just in a rut.

But then I thought back to the times I DID have motivation. what did I do back then? 

there was a brief period in 2025, around october, when I was incredibly consistent. I had watched one of leila hormozi's videos about systems, and how, to create a robust system, you need to start with something so easy *that you can't lose*. 

Remembering that key piece of info, I looked at my current lifts as well as my self-talk. Every time I went to the gym, I lifted light becasue I felt like I had no energy (even if I had caffeine). Sort of like going on a jog instead of a sprint outside. but then i thought subconsciously, "I am a loser for this because I'm not hitting a PR. I shouldn't even track it because it's junk volume." But I was no longer tracking my data, and i didnt stick to a plan, so i had no path to progress. and nothing to stick to, to say 'if i do this, i'm good.'. 

same way if i was running. well maybe according to my plan i only need to do an easy run today. or maybe it's a tempo run. but I wasn't tracking a plan like that, so i dind't like running either!

I think back to all the days in college when i meticulously tracked my walks across campus for *no reason*, other than I was obsessed with hitting the button. it did literally nothing for me. no use out of that data. and when i stopped, nothing changed. that's how useless it was. I felt the same way about tracking my lifts and using the app I created.

So it was a cycle of thoughts: One, i'm too tired to go insanely hard today. Two, The weight i'm lifting is really light. Three, I shouldn't even track this because its just junk volume and doesnt matter. Four, i should leave the gym because im not putting in any work and i dont want to anyway.


I realized that I needed a *system audit*.

Leila hormoi talked about her systems like they were boring, easy, and rewarding over time, because the progress compounds. And as I reflected on my system, i realized it sure was boring, but it wasn't easy or rewarding. 

There was one main culplit: the comparisons. I kept comparing my current progress to my all time highs. I realized that comparing myself to what some ghost of me did years ago is uselss. I learned that this stuff is like sports: the seasons reset every year, and you have a blank slate to go off of. You don't really get judged based on what last year's performance is, if a lot of things changed. Similary, bodybuilders in a bulk don't compare their bodyweight to peak competition weight and hate themselves for not being lean. They comapre themselves to their baseline at the start of the bulk. The start of every new phase, every season, every chapter, it has its own baseline. I can't compare myself to what a ghost of my past was doing.

so I had to make a new plan. I was going to start lifting on a fresh slate; all prior weights are disregarded. from week 1, all my lifts would be PRs. and my system, my *app*, needed to celebrate that. I needed to track and report on all my wins. Then, i needed to show my progress from the *start of the period*. this way, i can be focused on positive momentum rather than hating myself for not beating historiocal records. I'm not competing against some guy who was jacked up on adderall years ago. I'm competing against myself *right now*.

So all i had to do was build out that app.

### Will I stay sober past one year?
I'm not sure yet. For me, it was about getting healthier and getting rid of my craving to drink. A year later, I've achieved a lot of what i was hoping for. I want to keep this same relationship with alcohol going forward, but *with* the ability to have a drink if I want one.

It's also been incredible to see how much I got done with all the weekends I wasn't going out. I refined my [self-driving car project](https://sull1van.com/7500-miles-self-driving-car). I honed my health and got the [best blood test results](https://sull1van.com/best-liver-of-all-time) of my entire life. I worked on a passion project that [graded two dozen phone chargers](https://sull1van.com/a-dozen-chargers-in-the-lab), continuing a Google engineer's work. I created a half-dozen AI-assisted vibe coding projects, replacing stuff like my Spotify and my workout app with custom-coded alternatives that are 100% free to operate (article coming soon). A lot of stuff.


I plan to return to drinking casually one day. Maybe not just yet, but one day. And if I still can't do the 1-2 drinks a night thing, I've got a year's worth of sobriety showing me I can always do it again.
