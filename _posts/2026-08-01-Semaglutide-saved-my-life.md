In late 2022, I finally took steps to address my food addiction after five years. I had tried many things, and had good short-term success with them. One of them was "I can teach you to be thin" by Paul McKenna. That was one of the bright spots of 2022. For me it was never about being thin, only eating properly. But after a while, I succumbed to my cravings, and I took more drastic steps. I tried taking naltrexone to lessen the 'high' I got from enormous quantities of food. It helped a *little*, but it also blunted the effects of caffeine and adderall, and I needed those to study. So I stopped.

Eventually, it had gotten so bad that I checked myself into a treatment facility for my University's fall break. I packed my bags, told no one, and booked a flight to Minenapolis for the entirety of that long weekend. Over the next few days I heard stories from individuals who had dealt with food addiction throughout the course of their lives, most overweight but all equally as unhappy. I was the youngest in the room; most peolpe don't do something about this addiction until later in life. 

I loved my time there, and all the friends I made, but I knew that my solution wouldn't be like theirs. I say that in a neutral way: their solution worked for **them**, which is amazing. But for me, I knew that I planned to return to eating intuitively over the course of my life. And if I had plans of bodybuilding, or putting on any muscle at all, following their rigid schedules and plans wouldn't allow me to live as active a life as I envisioned. Therefore, I sought more and more help. 

I worked with Glenn Livingston , the author of "Never Binge again", with one on one coaching for several months. I knew my rules were absurdly strict at the get-go, but I was trying my best to limit myself from the cravings. ultimately, while I loved the philosophy, I knew I needed something more powerful. 

I turned to semaglutide. At the time, Ozempic and Wegovy had just started getting prescribed to the masses, and I found a lab online that manufactured it. They had lab tests proving it was pure, so I ordered from them. One night in early spring 2023, I reconstituted one of their vials and stuck it in.

The difference was night and day. 

I woke up with exactly zero craivngs. None. I honestly couldn't believe it. I started to walk around my house, look at the food that I had. I didn't want it. I didn't care about it. I could eat, or I could just... *not*. For someonne who had been fighting a losing battle with food for five years, it was an unimaginable change. 

I went back down to my bedroom and started to cry. 

The thing with food addiction is that it doesn't matter if you're full. Your hunger cravings mean NOTHING. Because your body things you are always starving, even when you're not. everything is fucked up and there is no way to stop. I tried so many things to stop the food. I tried making myself throw up, tried using laxatives to make it run through me. Take a double dose of adderall so the hunger went down. But with every single intervention, I always continued to eat. 

It didn't matter if I took adderall or laxatives to speed up my metabolism. As soon as the adderall or laxatives were done, my insides were empty, which meant plenty of room to stuff them with more food.

I'd buy entire boxes of ice cream sandwiches, knowing they'd be in my stomach before the night gave out. I looked at the snacks I had in my pantry, knowing that they would only last until I was hungry next. I lived with nine people at the time, and luckily (or unluckily) I was so good at hiding it that none of them knew. I'd retreat to my room when I overate. I'd bring my food with me, too. I wouldn't go out to shared dinners with others. As I'd been doing since high school, I binge ate so much every time that I was alone, that I would eat nothing when I'd go out with friends. "You're too healthy for us," they'd say as they bit into a burger or helped themselves to a side of fries. If only they knew that I ate so many calories before I saw them that I would physically explode if another one entered my body.

If you look at photos of me, you'd never know there was a problem. Actually, I looked decent the whole time. Why? Becasue I was forcing myself to get rid of as much food as I was eating. I'd make myself go on insane bouts of cardio, doing 1,000 calorie combo workouts at the gym, and going on long walks or runs in the evenings. Plus I'd take adderall and laxatives so that the food would exit my body faster. This combo was actually so effective for me that I got a hemmorhoid in 2021 that stuck with me until I got it removed four years later, in December of 2025. A permanent scar to remind me of what I once did.

But semaglutide changed all of this. Overnight, no cravings. *I ate normally*. 

Throughout the rest of 2023, I proceeded to weigh the exact same. Most people think users of semaglutide look bone thin and become unhealthy. Surely, some do, but I didn't I weighed the exact same.

I stayed on semaglutide for two years. Two years! It was actually summer of 2025 that I made the executive deicion to wean off. I had taken weeks off here and there, but they always ended with me binge eating. Semaglutide was the only thing that kept me from insatiable cravings. However, after two years, it seemed my body had almost started to lessen its own natural hunger signals. Also, I was having less fun at the gym, and I noticed that my lifts were no longer 'fun' like they were before semaglutide. Truth is, semaglutide takes your enjoyment out of everything you do, not just eating. So nothing gets you high.

Recognizing that I'd rather lift and deal with the cravings (depedning on how bad they are), I ultimately stopped taking semaglutide in summer 2025. And crazily enough, the cravings did not come back.

I immediately started a bulk aftter that, so I was eating extra calories. But i took out all the unehalhy food and focused on healthy foods for bulking. Then, in early 2026, when I came out of the bulk, I kept the same diet ! and as of mid-2026, **I have completely eliminated all sugar from my diet.** This is 100% of added sugar, and most natrual sugar as well.

it's genuineyl insane to see this transfromation. as it stands to day, i intuitively eat, and i don't try to lose weight anymore. I don't have cravings, and i don't eat sugar. It's incredible to see what semaglutide was able to do for me.

### Will I stay sober past one year?
I'm not sure yet. For me, it was about getting healthier and getting rid of my craving to drink. A year later, I've achieved a lot of what i was hoping for. I want to keep this same relationship with alcohol going forward, but *with* the ability to have a drink if I want one.

It's also been incredible to see how much I got done with all the weekends I wasn't going out. I refined my [self-driving car project](https://sull1van.com/7500-miles-self-driving-car). I honed my health and got the [best blood test results](https://sull1van.com/best-liver-of-all-time) of my entire life. I worked on a passion project that [graded two dozen phone chargers](https://sull1van.com/a-dozen-chargers-in-the-lab), continuing a Google engineer's work. A lot of stuff.


I plan to return to drinking casually one day. Maybe not just yet, but one day. And if I still can't do the 1-2 drinks a night thing, I've got a year's worth of sobriety showing me I can always do it again.
