One full year! Crazy to say it. Today officially marks one year since I went sober.

## What happened

I had toyed with the idea of going sober bascially ever since I graduated college. It just felt weird to be constantly drinking like I did in college, but with a real job and real adult responsibilities to deal with. It felt like that was only ever supposed to be a part of my life, and that I should move on. I kept having night after night where I got more drunk than I inteneded, and some concerning blood tests made me take my health more seriously. I wanted to drink less, but it felt too hard to moderate myself. So eventually I decided to just stop.

## The rules of my sobriety

Going sober was pretty daunting at first. I didn't really know what was in the bounds vs. out of the bounds of being sober. It sounds easy on the surface, but technicalities quickly arose as I got into it. For example, what if I drink a "non-alcoholic" beer that actually has 0.1% or 0.5% alcohol? 

At first, I was REALLY strict with it. But after some time, I realized that the 1 drop of alcohol inside an NA beer was never the problem I was trying to escape. I was literally just trying to stop getting drunk. So I made some rules:
1. If it's labeled "NA" (0.5% alcohol or less), I could drink it.
2. If something had true alcohol, I counted it as breaking sobriety if I swallowed it. 

That meant that the small alcohol my body got from drinking one of the NA beers was fine, and dipping my tongue to taste a friend's espresso martini is also fine (provided I didn't actually drink it).

You might be saying these are permissive rules, and I'd say that's fine. They worked for me. I was never in the territory of life-or-death alcoholism, so I didn't feel like my sobriety needed to be as strict. To those points above, I'm happy to say that I abided by them in totality for this whole year. I never once swallowed anything from an alcoholic beverage, I was never once drunk or buzzed in any way, and the only thing I ever drank aside from soda was an NA beer.

---

Another tenet of my sobriety was that it was actually threefold. It included **weed, nicotine, and alcohol**. To be honest, I don't know anyone who went sober from all three at the same time. But I know plenty of people who don't do any of these things, so I'm not special. I just wanted to make it a true sobriety for me, since I knew I'd get high every once in a while otherwise. The nicotine part was bonus. I've never been into nicotine, so going sober from it wasn't really my challenge.

## Just go longer

So while my sobriety was threefold, it was only alcohol that scared me. I told my parents and my friends I was doing it for two months, to see where things went. I didn't commit to going longer because I genuinely did not think it was possible. It seemed like the probability of relapse within two months was insanely high.

But we're here, so that definitely wasn't the case. It really started to take shape as I neared the halfway mark. One weekend I caught up with a friend, who offered what might be the single greatest piece of advice I heard throughout this entire journey. 

We got into chatting and I explained the sobriety and my two-month goal. He had been sober his entire life, and so he asked why I settled on two months.

"I don't know," I said. "I just thought that's the most I could do."

And then, this:

*"Why don't you just try to go longer?"*

Sometimes you really just need someone to say the obvious thing.

Just go longer. That's exactly what I did. As I approached the two-month mark, I said I'd extend it to six months. Then at six months, I was convinced I could do twelve. And here we are, twelve months later. It's crazy.

## What I actually miss

Here's the thing I keep coming back to: I don't miss being *drunk*. I miss that nice buzz that comes after one, maybe two drinks. I think back to college days, and how often I would go out, and while all of that was a lot of fun, I don't miss it. I know what's waiting for me at the bottom of a bottle of something strong, and it's a nasty hangover. Near the start of my sobriety, I could feel the hangovers kick in *while* I was still drunk. So drinking heavily has completely lost its appeal. But that nice state where you have the tiniest buzz from a drink THAT is the state I miss. 

That's been the center of this whole year. Not having alcohol is fine, I'll live, but I'd like to experience it again. Social commitments took on a flatter feeling - not significantly worse or anything, just... 10% less excitement, I'd say. The moments I miss are pretty specific and infrequent. You're at someone's house, out on a date, at a birthday dinner, etc. and one drink would be perfect to soften the mood.

But the vast majority of drinking? No. Going 100% sober is a great way to realize that most times you REALLY drank, you didn't need to. the 4+ drink kind of nights. Good riddance to those.


And this is why I wouldn't recommend going fully sober to most people. There's something to be said for having the opportunity to use things like alcohol when you want them. Same way you might want a sweet treat every once in a while, or maybe you want to have a a three-coffee day at work. In the same way, you might opt for a drink or two while you're out. For people who only drink occasionally, I don't see what you gain from stopping. A *lot* of people asked me that question, and while I feel like sobriety is awesome, I don't think everyone needs to do it. If you don't have a problem, you might not need to go off trying to fix it. That's my thougts at least.

## What changed

The changes in my body showed up fast, within ~3 months. I noticed increased energy during the day and whiter eyes. Honestly, a great combo. I love how white my eyes are now. I also worked on my general health and designed a custom supplement stack that I've been taking for about nine months now. These biomarkers trended to the best levels I've ever tested for:
- ALT
- AST
- HDL Cholesterol
- LDL Cholesterol
- Fasted Glucose
- Hemoglobin A1c

This was a really big accomplishment for me. The people close to me know my liver enzymes were elevated, indicative of alcohol-induced "liver stress". It was never bad enough to be liver disease, but it was getting close. I'm happy to say that, as of 2026, I've never had a better-functioning liver in my life. And that's saying a lot, since I have been getting these tests since I was seventeen!

Now, the alcohol cravings are definitely real, but those changed for me as well. Around the same time my liver began to improve, and my eyes whitened, the cravings evaporated. By month three I no longer had any big urge to drink. I'd settled into the reality that I'd just go to the bar with  friends, sit my ass down, and order a soda or NA beer. The lack of alcohol felt less like a craving and more like the addition of a new boring routine.
    And on that note: **NA beers are awesome.** They're not for everyone, but a ton of stores and bars have started stocking them in this last year. It definitely beats a soda if you are doing something social and want a drink. Also, if you aren't a beer person, NA beers might make you one after you go sober, lol.




## What I learned about people

People ask constant questions, and most of them are supportive. I've had tons of questions about my sobriety and never once been shamed for it. What I do get, sometimes, is a quiet guilt. I'll see other people drinking and they almost seem upset that I'm not. Almost like how it would be if you went out for a friend's birthday, and you brought your own food because you're on a meal plan. It breaks social norms and confuses people. The alternative is to stay home and skip social events, which is a terrible alternative.

The thing to actually watch out for is the opposite: people who *encouraged* me to drink back when I was trying to cut down. I'd explained that goal to most of the people around me, and they were supportive, but some would still ask me to get extremely drunk together. I'm not mad about it, that's what I was known for up until 2025. But it's just a weird feeling to push yourself in one direction after being known for the opposite, and find the people around you aren't used to it or serious about it. I don't hold it against anyone. I was never that public about just how important it was to me.

I'll also say there were _countless_ people who were incredibly supportive. People who'd start buying me my coveted NA beers, and people who were just perfectly comfortable going out with me even if I wasn't drinking. That meant a lot.

## Will I stay sober past one year?

As of right now, I haven't broken sobriety. But that could change. I achieved pretty much everything I was looking for this year, and I don't have anything left to prove to myself by going longer. Now I know that if I can make a decision like this before, I can do it again. It was really about convincing myself that I *could*, and resetting my relationship with alcohol. 

It's also been incredible to see how much I could accomplish in the in-between, having repurposed all the weekends I wasn't going out. I refined my self-driving car project. I honed my health and got the best blood test results of my entire life. I worked on a passion project project that graded two dozen phone chargers, continuing a Google engineer's work. I created a half-dozen AI-assisted vibe coding projects, replacing stuff like my Spotify and my workout app with custom-coded alternatives that are 100% free to operate (article coming soon). A lot of stuff.

Anyway, I plan to return to drinking casually one day. But not yet. I'm too curious about what I can build in the meantime.
